# M3 results: one round of reflection, six setups

**PR:** [reflective-qa#16](https://github.com/bhavya94/reflection-agent-for-paper-qa/pull/16) · **Code at:** [`42f655b`](https://github.com/bhavya94/reflection-agent-for-paper-qa/tree/42f655bda7a66ee1ad2d8b30f9a3a7cb1bfd1b91)

## Summary
M3 asked whether one round of reflection improves grounded answers on QASPER. Six setups revised the M1 drafts of the
same 200 questions: code feedback, self-critique, and a stronger separate critic. **None changed answer-F1 measurably**;
every 95% interval includes zero. That isn't because nothing happened. Two real effects cancel: critics fix wrong
"Unanswerable" answers, and they make answers that were already given worse by rewording them. The experiment cost
$2.29; the whole project so far, $3.77 of a $20 budget.

## Results (200 questions, answer-F1)

## What it means
1. **The critic finds the right problem.** M1's top lead was wrong abstention. Every critic setup turned wrong
   "Unanswerable" answers into answers, and those revisions gained +0.07 to +0.18 answer-F1. Abstention fell from 29.5%
   to 19% for Haiku self-critique.
2. **Rewording hurts.** When a critic rewrites an answer that was already given, it usually loses word overlap with the
   annotators: −0.07 to −0.14 in three of four critic setups. These revisions are often reasonable, but they're longer
   or worded differently.
3. **Shortening is the one clean win.** Code feedback only shortened long answers. On those 15 Sonnet answers it gained
   +0.106, but it touched too few to move the total.
4. **Noise is about ±0.02.** The last two setups are near-replicates (the checks flag almost no Haiku drafts), and they
   ended 0.020 apart. Smaller differences between setups mean nothing at this size.
5. **The stronger critic isn't better here.** Sonnet critiquing Haiku cost about 16 times Haiku self-critique and
   scored the same.

## Caveats
- **Answer-F1 rewards word overlap.** A correct revision worded differently from the annotators scores low, so some
  "worse" rewordings may be fine answers. Checking that needs human labels, which is the offline-eval project.
- **The revise prompt was adjusted** after a 20-question pilot drawn from the same 200 questions. The change only
  restates rules the writer already had.
- **One revise round** only, on one dataset and two models.

## Decision log

|---|---|---|---|---|

| Headline metric | Answer-F1; abstention rate; better/worse counts | Answer-F1, with the others beside it | It's the official QASPER metric, comparable with M1; the others explain why it didn't move | Human labels exist |

## Next version (suggested)
Keep what helped and drop what hurt: let the critic flag only wrong abstentions and over-long answers, and keep any
draft that already gives an answer unless it is unsupported. Cost would be close to Haiku self-critique ($0.03 for 200
questions), and the results page would add it as a new row.
